Understanding bank fees

Overdraft charges and international transfer costs — what they mean, when they may apply, how they are calculated, and how to verify them with your own bank.

What does “overdraft” mean?

An overdraft (découvert bancaire) occurs when the balance of a bank account falls below zero: money leaves the account (a payment, a withdrawal, a transfer) while there is not enough available funds to cover it. The bank therefore lends the missing amount — either because it has authorised this possibility in advance (agreed overdraft) or because the operation went through anyway (unagreed overdraft).

An overdraft is a form of credit. Like any credit, it can generate costs: interest on the borrowed amount and, in some cases, handling charges.

In which circumstances may a bank apply fees?

  • Agreed overdraft: interest is usually charged on the negative balance, for each day the account remains overdrawn, at the rate set in your contract.
  • Unagreed overdraft / exceeded limit: when the authorised limit is exceeded, the bank may apply a penalty fee, in addition to interest.
  • Rejected or returned operation: a payment bounced for insufficient funds may be charged as a “returned item” or “unpaid item” fee.
  • Delayed or failed transfer: additional costs may arise if an international transfer is recalled, rejected by a correspondent bank, or returned to the sender.
  • Currency conversion: an exchange-rate margin may be applied when the transfer is converted into another currency.
